Speaker notes · How attribution works today

The current process is more structured than last touch. It first builds candidate links through customer, application and DAB references, then uses source, product and time rules. The winner is chosen through a hierarchy of priorities; recency comes later in that hierarchy. This makes production reporting reproducible. Its limitation is that a winner does not tell us how several interactions contributed, how sensitive that conclusion is to the chosen window, or whether the customer would have converted anyway. The audit has traced both the winning row and the separately constructed reported path. Neither should be described as the entire customer journey.

Evidence and scope: Current report: production attribution mechanism, screenshots 1000036756–1000036758. Exact ranking hierarchy is in appendix B.

LUMEN AI · INTERNAL9 / 11

Speaker notes · From sharing credit to understanding contribution

Fractional attribution answers an allocation question: how to divide credit across eligible observed interactions. The next question is causal: what would have changed if the channel interaction had not happened? Customers are already different before a touchpoint; their existing need, intent and relationship influence both their channel activity and conversion likelihood. Bringing that context into the analysis can improve how we interpret channel contribution. We do not need to introduce a specific estimator in this executive discussion. The recent report explicitly says the historical estimates do not establish incrementality. Experiments or a defensible comparison design are needed for that stronger conclusion. Later work can also model the probability that a touchpoint occurred but was not observed; those estimates must retain uncertainty rather than being treated as known events.

Evidence and scope: Current report: historical same-window benchmark caveats and readiness, 1000036772 and 1000036775. Future missing-touchpoint modeling is a proposed extension, not an achieved result.

Appendix C · how to read the findings

The audit separates several kinds of missing evidence

These distinctions prevent an apparent channel difference from becoming a misleading business conclusion.

Identity

Some interactions cannot be connected to a person, application or conversion.

Normalize placeholder keys before matching.

Product definitions

Interaction and conversion labels do not always describe LOBs the same way.

Some zero same-LOB rates are taxonomy artifacts.

Timing and source coverage

History length, source start dates and business anchors change what is visible.

Compare consistent periods and definitions.

Shared evidence

One event or identifier may connect to several conversions or customers.

Resolve ambiguity before assigning credit.

LUMEN AI · INTERNALAPPENDIX C

Speaker notes · The audit separates several kinds of missing evidence

The audit found 849,587 conversion identifiers, or 6.62%, linking to multiple customer identities, with a maximum of 588. Joint applications can explain some multiplicity, but the tail requires decomposition before labels are fixed. Placeholder application keys XYZ and ABC and DAB keys NO_DAB and NO_DAB_CONV must be treated as missing. The quarterly collision measures use compressed evidence groups, so they are not exact physical-event collision rates. Prepared history has already passed source and policy choices; it is not all raw history. These distinctions explain why adding more history alone cannot solve the complete attribution problem.
